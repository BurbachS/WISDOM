Introduction back to ToC

Wearable devices have become an important technology for monitoring physiological and behavioral indicators associated with human stress. Modern stress studies frequently combine data from multiple sensors, such as photoplethysmography (PPG), electrodermal activity (EDA), accelerometers, and temperature sensors, together with contextual information, experimental protocols, and human annotations. However, these datasets are often stored in heterogeneous formats with limited semantic interoperability, making data integration, reuse, and cross-study analysis challenging. The Wearable Integrated Stress Data Ontology Model (WISDOM) addresses these challenges by providing a semantic model for representing stress-related concepts and wearable sensing data in a machine-interpretable manner. The ontology captures experimental context, stressors, stress conditions, activities, wearable sensors, observable properties, physiological recordings, time-series observations, and annotation records while maintaining explicit semantic relationships between these entities. Rather than replacing existing ontologies, WISDOM extends and integrates well-established Semantic Web vocabularies. SOSA/SSN provides the foundation for modeling sensors and observations, OWL-Time represents temporal information, QUDT supports measurement units, and Schema.org is used for describing study participants. This reuse of established standards promotes interoperability with existing knowledge graphs and facilitates the integration of heterogeneous stress datasets.
